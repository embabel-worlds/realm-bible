How it works

The data

Everything comes from realm-bible in this world:

  • Book, Passage, Verse — stored in the graph: 66 books, 1,189 chapters (a passage is a chapter), 31,102 verses. Each verse carries the King James text and the World English Bible (a modern, public-domain translation). From Theographic Bible Metadata (CC BY-SA 4.0) and eBible.org.
  • Documents — each chapter of the World English Bible is also ingested as a searchable document, chunked and embedded, declared in the realm's sources.yml and kept current by the appliance. The King James chapters are ingested too, but the search reads the WEB (below). Only these Bible documents are searched — other documents in the world are excluded inside the retrieval.
  • BiblePerson — fetched, not stored, from Theographic's People.csv the first time a chapter's people are asked for. Theographic marks much of its data unfinished; a person's tags can be wrong.
  • Five older translations (ASV, Basic English, Darby, Douay-Rheims, Young's Literal) — fetched live from bible-api.com, one verse at a time, only when you open a verse.

The questions

SearchPassages — which chapters narrate or contain what you described. Parameters: query (your words), limit 8. It is an agentic search: a bounded model loop reformulates your description, runs both a meaning-based and a word-based retrieval over the Bible's World English chapters, reads into the candidates, and keeps only the chapters it judges to fit — citing only text it actually read. Each kept chapter is mapped back to its passage (a document's uri ends in the chapter's reference) and returned with every verse in both translations, so switching KJV/WEB and opening a chapter cost no further calls:

MATCH (c:Concept {value: $query})-[r:RELEVANT_TO {via: 'agentic-rag',
      intent: 'find the Bible chapter that narrates or contains what the reader describes'}]->(d:Document)
WHERE 'web' IN d.tags
WITH r, d, split(d.uri, '/')[3] AS osis
MATCH (b:Book)<-[:IN]-(p:Passage {osis: osis})<-[:IN]-(v:Verse)
WITH r, p, b, v ORDER BY v.verse
WITH r, p, b, collect({n: v.verse, kjv: v.text, web: v.web, ref: v.name}) AS verses
RETURN r.rank AS rank, r.score AS fit, p.osis AS osis, p.name AS passage,
       b.testament AS testament, r.snippet AS matched, verses
ORDER BY rank LIMIT $limit

Why the World English Bible is searched even when you read the King James: you describe a passage in modern English, so it is matched against modern English. Measured on "a woman kills a sleeping enemy general by driving a tent peg through his head": the WEB ranks Judges 4 and Judges 5 first and second; the KJV, where she "smote the nail into his temples", misses both in its top five.

Why agentic: you describe a story, not its words. "Bears eat boys who mocked a prophet" shares almost no vocabulary with 2 Kings 2 ("youths… mocked him… two female bears… mauled"); a single retrieval pass ranked it eighth, behind chapters about eating. The agentic search puts it first — as it does Jonah 1, Luke 10 (the Good Samaritan) and 1 Samuel 17 described without names, all of which the single pass missed. It takes 4–8 seconds instead of under one.

fit is the search's own judgement, 0 to 1, of how well a chapter fits; 1.0 means it read the chapter and judged that it narrates or contains what you described. It is shown only when below 1.0. The highlighted verses are the evidence the search read — found on this page by locating each verse's WEB wording in that evidence.

PeopleInPassage — who a chapter mentions, when you open it. Parameter: passage (e.g. "Judges 4").

MATCH (:Passage {name: $passage})<-[:IN]-(v:Verse)-[:MENTIONS]->(p:BiblePerson)
RETURN p.displayTitle AS person, p.personLookup AS id,
       count(v) AS verses, collect(v.verse) AS verseNumbers
ORDER BY verses DESC, person

CompareTranslations — one verse in seven translations, when you click a verse. Parameter: ref (e.g. "Judges 4:21"). The King James and World English come from the graph; the other five are fetched live for that verse.

When it looks wrong

  • No chapters. The search found nothing it judged to fit — it returns only chapters it judged, not the nearest regardless. Describe it another way: what happens, who is there, a phrase you remember.
  • One chapter, not eight. Normal: only chapters judged to fit come back.
  • A wrong chapter. The judgement is a model's; the highlighted verses show what it read, so you can see why it chose it.
  • No highlighted verses on a result. The matched excerpt could not be lined up with single verses; the first verses of the chapter are shown instead, and the whole chapter is in the reader.
  • "People" is empty. Theographic tags no one in that chapter — common in law, poetry and genealogy-free prophecy — not an error.
  • Fewer than seven translations. Young's Literal is New Testament only on its source, so an Old Testament verse has six. A missing ASV, Basic English, Darby or Douay-Rheims row means that fetch failed; its reason is shown.
  • Search fails outright. The appliance needs an embedding model to search by meaning; without one, documents exist but cannot be searched this way.

What made this possible

  • The corpus is declared, not coded: a lane: documents entry in the realm's sources.yml renders each chapter from the stored verses, and the appliance ingests, embeds, and re-ingests only chapters whose text changed.
  • An agentic retrieval and the graph are one query: SearchPassages runs the retrieval loop as an edge (RELEVANT_TO {via: 'agentic-rag'}) and then traverses Book ← Passage ← Verse in the same Cypher — elsewhere that is an agent framework, a vector store and a database joined in application code.
  • People are never stored: MENTIONS is a join to a published CSV, fetched when a chapter asks for it.
  • This page has no backend of its own. It asks the world for three views by name when you act.
